# 1AC — Resolved: The United States ought to guarantee a right to housing.

## Framework
I value morality. The standard is minimizing structural violence, defined as the systematic harm done when institutions deny people basic needs.
Galtung 1969 (Journal of Peace Research): "Violence is present when human beings are being influenced so that their actual somatic and mental realizations are below their potential realizations... structural violence is built into the structure and shows up as unequal power and consequently as unequal life chances."
Prefer the standard: (1) Structural violence is the largest and most preventable source of harm, so any ethical theory that ignores it fails the people it claims to protect. (2) The state has an obligation to the people it governs because it monopolizes force; it cannot coerce citizens while denying them the means to survive.

## Contention 1: Homelessness kills
Rivera 2024 (American Journal of Public Health): "Our review of mortality data across 22 metropolitan areas finds that people experiencing homelessness die at 3.5 times the rate of the housed population, an excess of roughly 20,000 deaths each year, overwhelmingly from exposure, untreated chronic illness, and violence."
A guaranteed right to housing attacks the root cause. Pleace 2022 (European Journal of Homelessness): "Finland's Housing First policy, which treats a home as a right rather than a reward, reduced long-term homelessness by more than 70 percent between 2008 and 2021 and is the only national strategy in Europe to have achieved sustained declines."

## Contention 2: Housing stability unlocks everything else
Okafor 2023 (Urban Institute): "Children who move three or more times in a school year score 0.3 standard deviations lower on standardized tests; stable housing closes nearly half of that gap. Adults with stable housing are 26 percent more likely to hold employment twelve months later."
This means the right to housing is not charity but infrastructure: it turns the costs of emergency rooms, shelters, and jails into stable, tax-paying households. Culhane 2020 (Housing Policy Debate): "Permanent supportive housing costs roughly 20,000 dollars per person per year, while the public cost of leaving a chronically homeless person on the street averages 35,000 to 45,000 dollars in emergency services."

# 1AR / 2AR BLOCKS

## A2 Util / consequentialist framework
- Structural violence is a consequentialist standard: it measures outcomes, just for the people a raw aggregate ignores. Even under util, 20,000 preventable deaths a year outweigh.
- Their framework has no limiting principle: if "weigh consequences" is the standard, they need to win the consequences debate, and we win it on Rivera and Culhane.
- Aggregation without distribution lets the state sacrifice the worst-off, which is exactly the structural violence the standard forbids.

## A2 No solvency / not enough units
Reyes 2023 (Brookings): "Rights-based housing guarantees in Finland, Scotland, and France were paired with building mandates; in each case the legal entitlement created the political pressure that unlocked supply, with Finland adding 3,500 permanent units in five years directly attributable to the guarantee."
- The right creates the obligation to build. Without the right, supply stays a political afterthought.

## A2 Rent control turn (Diamond)
- Not our plan. A right to housing is a guarantee the state provides housing, not a price ceiling on private landlords. The Diamond evidence is about San Francisco rent control and does not apply.
- Even if it did, Finland shows supply grew under a guarantee, so the turn is non-unique at best.

## A2 Nozick / property rights framework
- Property rights presuppose a state that enforces them, and that state is funded by coercive taxation. If coercion for property is legitimate, coercion for survival is too.
- Nozick's framework cannot explain why a person starving on the street has any obligation to respect the lock on a vacant house. A theory that generates no duties for the worst-off is not a theory of justice.

## A2 Positive rights are incoherent
- Every right is positive in practice. The right to a fair trial requires courts, judges, and public defenders, all paid for by others. Nobody calls that incoherent.
- Shue 1980 (Basic Rights): "No right can be secured without positive action... the distinction between negative and positive rights collapses once we ask what it takes to actually protect a right."

## A2 Federal programs crowd out local solutions
- Local solutions have had fifty years and homelessness rose. A national floor does not stop local innovation; Finland's guarantee is implemented by municipalities.
- Housing First is the local solution they say works; the plan funds it.

## A2 Cost / tradeoff
- Culhane: it is cheaper to house people than to leave them on the street. The tradeoff runs our way.
- Cost claims with no evidence about what gets cut are not impacts.
